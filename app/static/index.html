What the sounds look like

Per-class averages over all 400 clips in the dataset (error bars: one standard deviation across clips).

Active ratio

Share of the clip where loudness is above 10% of its peak

Story: rain, helicopter, sea waves and chainsaw fill almost the entire 5 seconds, while sneezing, dog and rooster are short events surrounded by silence. This splits the classes into continuous and impulsive sounds. It is also why the model must be careful with silence padding.

Spectral centroid (Hz)

The "centre of mass" of the spectrum, a measure of brightness

Story: rain is the brightest sound (about 2.7 kHz), while dog, helicopter, rooster and sneezing sit near 1.1-1.2 kHz. Rain is easy to separate from helicopter, but those low-centroid classes overlap, so confusion among them is the most likely.

RMS energy

Average loudness of the clip

Story: the clock tick is about 10x quieter than the helicopter or chainsaw. Loudness is informative, but the model standardises each clip, so it does not depend on microphone volume.

Zero-crossing rate

How often the waveform crosses zero: a proxy for noisiness

Story: noisy, hissing sounds (rain, chainsaw, crying baby) cross zero far more often than tonal or low-frequency ones (helicopter, dog).

1. Upload new labelled clips

Select several .wav files that all belong to the same class. They are decoded, normalised to 16 kHz mono 5 s, and stored for retraining.

2. Retrain

Fine-tunes the live model on the original training data plus every upload. The new model goes live only if its validation F1 is not worse than the current one. It also starts automatically once 30 new clips are waiting.
